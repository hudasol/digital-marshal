## How the two models differ

| Aspect | Point queue (WP2.7 and WP2.9) | SUMO prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles | 10, 24 or 60 abstract vehicles | 7.5 SUMO vehicles per abstract vehicle (75, 180, 450), so that one abstract vehicle per minute corresponds to 450 vehicles per hour |
| Randomness | None. Deterministic. | Seeded: randomised departure jitter and driver speed factor. 10 to 20 seeds per cell. |
| Queueing | FIFO service with fixed headway per edge, infinite storage, no spillback | Car following with lanes; discharge rates are measured by calibration, not assumed |
| Capacity | Vehicles per minute per edge | Lane counts (Route A one lane, Route B two lanes) plus measured discharge |
| Travel time | Departure to exit of the last edge, censored at the horizon | Scheduled departure to arrival, so insertion delay at the origin counts; censored at the horizon |
| Horizon | 7200 s (explicit contract, `docs/decisions/ADR-0001-horizon.md`) | 3600 s in the runner. This is a known mismatch (open item). |
| Closure | Edges E2 and E3 closed from 120 s; route legality audited from records | Same edges closed; routes through them are rejected at assignment and audited from the trace |
| Routing | Route locked at P, decisions on a 30 s grid anchored at 180 s | Decisions on a 30 s tick, vehicles within a lead time of P |
| Independent policy | Frozen semantics: first vehicle of a batch probes and the batch copies its choice | Each vehicle decision uses a shared predictor; see the route counts below |

## What can and cannot be concluded

1. **The two models do not use identical policy semantics.** In the manageable scenario the frozen point queue independent policy sends all vehicles to Route A, while the SUMO independent policy splits them (46.8 on A and 28.2 on B on average, from `sumo_results.csv`). The policies share names, not definitions.
2. **The 1.70% and 0.53% figures are not a replication pair.** They compare coordinated against differently defined independent policies, in a deterministic model and a seeded model. Comparability has not been established, so the difference between them should not be interpreted as an effect of model fidelity.
3. **Queueing at moderate load differs in sign.** In the manageable scenario the point queue shows no difference between policies, while SUMO shows static Route A better than splitting policies. This indicates that the FIFO point queue overstates queueing at moderate load. This is an observation, not a calibration, and parameters were not tuned to force agreement.
4. **Totals are not directly comparable.** SUMO totals divided by 7.5 are higher than the point queue totals, which is expected from insertion delay, acceleration and junction effects. Only relative comparisons within a model are meaningful.
5. **The ranking of policies is consistent across models in the near capacity and overload scenarios** (coordinated at or below independent, and both below static), but the size of the gap is small in both and is far below the provisional 5% target relative to the independent baseline.
6. **Overload in SUMO is not comparable.** The origin saturates and vehicles wait to be inserted, so overload totals mix origin delay with route choice effects.
7. **Statistical statements apply to SUMO only.** The point queue is deterministic, so no significance is inferred from it. SUMO intervals are bootstrap intervals over matched seeds, from a prototype that has not been independently reviewed.
