## Reading the tables

1. **Completion and safety.** All 300 SUMO runs are `ok`: every trip completed, and the trace shows no closed-link entries, no transitions outside the network connections, no route outside A or B, and no collisions. The point-queue frozen runs also pass their gates.
2. **Near capacity.** SUMO coordinated batch improves on `independent_periodic` by 0.53% (bootstrap 95% interval 0.12 to 0.90, better in 16 of 20 seeds) and on the frozen-rule comparator `independent_batch_copy` by 0.56%. The point queue gives 1.70%. Same sign, smaller size in SUMO. **Neither meets the provisional 5% target**, and nothing was changed to move either number.
3. **Half capacity** is the closest agreement: 1.20% in the point queue, 1.03% (0.68 to 1.42, 10 of 10 seeds) in SUMO. Both models also rank static last.
4. **Double capacity and manageable load.** The point queue sends every vehicle to route A under all three policies (improvement 0.00%). In SUMO, coordinated and both independent comparators are within 0.1% of each other, consistent with "no difference", but all three are worse than static route A, by 4.1% (manageable) and 9.9% (double). The decision log shows the cause is in the shared SUMO predictor: in the double-capacity run (seed 1) it rates route A about 25 s slower than B for the first vehicle at the 210 s tick, so most of that cohort goes to B, and static all-A finishes faster. The predictor overstates the route A delay at this load. Why it does is not isolated. Changing it would change the policies, so Track C does not; it is listed as follow-up engineering.
5. **Overload is non-comparable.** SUMO origin insertion delay reaches 61 s on average under some policies, so SUMO totals there mix origin queueing with route choice. The improvement (1.18%, interval 0.02 to 2.43) is reported but not compared with the point queue.
6. **Horizon.** Both models used 7200 s here. No SUMO trip arrives after 3600 s, so every SUMO number above would be identical at 3600 s, and the historical prototype (run at 3600 s) is reproduced exactly. The horizon choice (D1) does not change any current result.
7. **What the labels mean.** "Partially comparable" means the scenario, closure, horizon and travel-time window match and SUMO is not origin-limited, but the models, the batching rule and (for half and double) the capacity mechanism differ. No scenario is "comparable": that would need identical comparator semantics and batching, which neither SUMO comparator has. SUMO totals are about 15% above the point queue after dividing by 7.5 (insertion, acceleration, junctions); only relative comparisons within a model are meaningful.
8. **Statistics apply to SUMO only.** The point queue is deterministic. The SUMO intervals come from 10 or 20 matched seeds of a synthetic, unreviewed prototype and say nothing about real traffic.
